# Source Atlas

**Seed list; last checked:** 2026-11-20. These are starting points, not an exhaustive bibliography, endorsement, or substitute for source appraisal. Confirm current versions and access before relying on them.

## Comparative and historical research

- Human Relations Area Files, **eHRAF World Cultures**: ethnographic collections organized by culture and subject; access is membership-based. Useful infrastructure, not a complete or neutral representation of all cultures. https://hraf.yale.edu/products/ehraf-world-cultures/index.html
- V-Dem Institute, **Datasets**: comparative political indicators and documentation. Inspect operational definitions, coverage, uncertainty, and version before use. https://www.v-dem.net/data/
- Nobel Prize, **Elinor Ostrom: facts and Nobel lecture**: starting point for commons and institutional diversity; read original scholarship and critiques in context. https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/facts/ and https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/lecture/

## AI governance, rights, and standards

- UNESCO, **Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence** (2021). Intergovernmental normative framework; distinguish recommendation from binding domestic law. https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence
- OECD, **AI Principles** (adopted 2019, updated 2024). Read the principles and implementation material; not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific law. https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles
- NIST, **AI Risk Management Framework** (2023) and its official resource pages. Voluntary framework; assess scope and version. https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- Council of Europe, **Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law** (CETS 225). Check ratification, entry-into-force, and implementation status at research time. https://www.coe.int/en/web/artificial-intelligence/the-framework-convention-on-artificial-intelligence
- EU, **Artificial Intelligence Act**, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Consult the official legal text and current implementation/timeline information. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng

## Work, participation, and well-being

- International Labour Organization, **Social dialogue**: institutions and processes of tripartite and bipartite consultation; assess local context and worker representation. https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/social-dialogue
- OECD, **How's Life? / Well-being framework**: multidimensional measures of current and future well-being. Check latest edition and indicator definitions. https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/measuring-well-being-and-progress.html
- UNDP, **Human Development Reports and data**: human development measures and methodological documentation. Check latest edition and indicator definitions. https://hdr.undp.org/data-center

## Bounded and episodic self-governance

These sources seed the new scope; they are not a complete bibliography or interchangeable evidence across cases. Read movement rules and participant accounts alongside independent scholarship, archival records, and critical accounts. Citation numbers correspond to `research/sources.jsonl` for this revision; IDs are local to this research session.

- Fabian Frenzel, Anna Feigenbaum, and Patrick McCurdy, “Protest Camps: An Emerging Field of Social Movement Research,” *The Sociological Review* 62(3), 2014. Comparative conceptual framework for studying camp infrastructures, including governance, communication, action, and domestic/social-reproduction functions; not itself an evaluation of every camp. https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-954X.12111 [1]
- Patrick McCurdy, Anna Feigenbaum, and Fabian Frenzel, “Protest Camps and Repertoires of Contention,” *Social Movement Studies*, 2016. Examines how protest camps facilitate development, testing, and diffusion of tactics; distinguish its conceptual contribution from case-specific evidence. https://doi.org/10.1080/14742837.2015.1037263 [12]
- NYU Tamiment Library / Robert F. Wagner Labor Archives, **How to get a Proposal to the General Assembly**, Occupy Wall Street Archives Working Group Records, circa 2011–2012. Primary archival handout documenting the proposal process and progressive stack; represents a procedural artifact, not proof that practice matched the guide. https://specialcollections.hosting.nyu.edu/exhibitions/occupy/items/OWS025 [2]
- Ruth Kinna, Alex Prichard, and Thomas Swann, “Occupy and the Constitution of Anarchy,” *Global Constitutionalism* 8(2), 2019. Comparative analysis of General Assembly minutes from Wall Street, Oakland, and London; useful for institutional rules, but minutes cannot establish every participant’s views or experience. https://doi.org/10.1017/S204538171900008X [3]
- Gianmarco Savio, “Coordination outside formal organization: consensus-based decision-making and occupation in the Occupy Wall Street movement,” *Contemporary Justice Review*, 2015. Ethnographic study of autonomy, assemblies, informal decision-making, and coordination in OWS. https://doi.org/10.1080/10282580.2015.1005509 [4]
- Andrew Cornell, “Occupy Wall Street and Consensus Decision Making: Historicizing the Preoccupation With Process,” *Social Text: Periscope*, 2013. Historical and critical discussion of consensus; an interpretive essay, not a neutral procedural manual. https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/D8902DXN [5]
- Leon N. Kunz, “Strategic and prefigurative deliberation in Hong Kong’s pro-democracy movement,” *Social Movement Studies* (published online 17 February 2025). Interview-based comparison of Occupy Central’s planned assemblies and the Umbrella occupations’ less structured deliberative spaces. https://doi.org/10.1080/14742837.2025.2461502 [15]
- Jeffie Po, “The Problem of Legitimacy in the Hong Kong Umbrella Movement,” *Discover Society*, 2016. Interview-based discussion of representation and coordination problems after a planned campaign became a multi-site occupation; a short research commentary, not a comprehensive case study. https://archive.discoversociety.org/2016/04/05/the-problem-of-legitimacy-in-the-hong-kong-umbrella-movement [13]
- Ho Man Leung, *Repertoire, Framing and Visions: The Occupy Movement in Hong Kong*, MPhil thesis, Lingnan University, 2016. Interview-based thesis that distinguishes movement factions and internal conflict; useful as a research lead, not a sole account. https://commons.ln.edu.hk/soc_etd/40 [6]
- Michael Seidman, “Workers in a Repressive Society of Seductions: Parisian Metallurgists in May-June 1968,” *French Historical Studies* 18(1), 1993. Historical account emphasizing workers’ varied priorities and workplace conditions; useful against collapsing the French events into a single student or self-management narrative. https://doi.org/10.2307/286966 [8]
- Sojourner Truth Organization, **The Occupation of Factories in France: May 1968**, retrospective inquiry based on interviews with union officials, militants, and other sources. An openly political source with a selected-factory sample; use as a perspective and lead, not a representative survey. https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/sojournertruth/gsif.html [7]
- Sam Dolgoff (ed.), *The Anarchist Collectives: Workers’ Self-Management in the Spanish Revolution, 1936–1939*. A sympathetic anarchist collection and historical account; pair with archival, labor, and critical scholarship, and distinguish the Spanish Revolution’s particular conditions from contemporary protest collectives. https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/sam-dolgoff-editor-the-anarchist-collectives [9]
- “Collectivisations in Catalonia and the Region of Valencia during the Spanish Civil War, 1936–1939,” *Catalan Historical Review*. Historical synthesis that notes uneven evidence, contested accounts, worker committees, and the changing relation to unions and state authorities; use to counter romanticized or purely top-down accounts. https://www.raco.cat/index.php/CatalanHistoricalReview/article/download/96853/426261 [14]

## Research practice

For every citation, record the exact item used, date accessed, version, source type, relevant evidence, and limitations in `templates/source-record.md`. Follow `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`. Prefer primary texts for institutional rules and authoritative current sources for law or standards; seek independent scholarship and affected-party perspectives for interpretation and impact.
