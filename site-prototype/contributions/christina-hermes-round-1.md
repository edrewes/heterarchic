# Independent site contribution — Christina / Hermes

**Status:** Proposal for synthesis; not a settled project position.

## One content/interaction idea: an evidence-layered case pane

For each case, let visitors switch among three clearly labeled layers:

1. **Documented record** — what a contemporaneous rule, archive, dataset, or directly sourced account says.
2. **Interpretations and disputes** — what researchers or participants infer, including disagreement and the evidence each interpretation uses.
3. **Open questions / design hypotheses** — what remains unknown and what a present-day design might test; visibly marked as a proposal, not a historical finding.

Keep place, dates, actors, and source links attached to each layer. An original diagram can make a mechanism legible, but should be labeled interpretive rather than presented as archival imagery. This should make the site inviting without letting a visual metaphor collapse intended rules, observed practice, and evaluation into one claim.

## One case/mechanism: Occupy Wall Street’s progressive stack

Feature the progressive stack as a facilitation practice within New York’s 2011 General Assembly process: a useful example for examining how a meeting’s speaking queue may try to address unequal voice, and how informal facilitation itself exercises power. An NYU archive preserves an OWS proposal-process handout that describes the intended procedure; it is evidence of a documented procedural artifact, not proof that the practice was consistently used or improved participation.[1]

Pair that artifact with Kinna, Prichard, and Swann’s analysis of General Assembly minutes, Savio’s ethnographic work on coordination outside formal organization, and Cornell’s historical critique of consensus. These sources differ in method and should not be treated as interchangeable or as direct measures of the stack’s effects.[2][3][4]

**Interpretation to test, not a finding:** a queue rule can make voice disparities more visible, but its actual effects depend on who sets categories and speaking order, how facilitators apply the rule, meeting accessibility and duration, and what decisions remain outside the assembly. Formal inclusion intent alone cannot establish equal influence.

## Evidence needed before making stronger claims

- The archival procedure document and other contemporaneous, site-specific records, with dates and provenance.
- Minutes, recordings, or participant accounts that reveal actual facilitation practice and variation over time—not just the intended rules.
- Perspectives from participants who were intended to benefit, alongside dissenting and critical accounts, including people who could not attend or speak.
- Evidence about agenda control, speaking opportunities, meeting burden, decision follow-through, and informal influence; avoid using speaking turns alone as a proxy for power or outcomes.
- Clear separation between Zuccotti Park’s New York practice and other Occupy sites or movements.

**Uncertainty / limits:** The available source leads establish that a procedure was documented and that scholarship examines OWS deliberation and coordination; they do not by themselves establish the progressive stack’s causal effect on inclusion, legitimacy, or outcomes. The case should remain bounded to a particular place and period, and the page should show counterevidence and missing perspectives when found.
