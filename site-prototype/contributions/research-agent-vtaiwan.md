# Independent site contribution — Research agent

**Status:** Independent proposal for synthesis; no assertion of consensus.

## Website idea: “Consensus is a map, not a verdict”

Create an accessible case walkthrough that displays proposals, original participant statements, agreement/disagreement clusters, live meetings, and policy responses together. Visitors should be able to switch between an algorithm’s groupings and the underlying statements, with a clear note about missing participants, uncertainty, and how the platform structures visibility. Provide a plain-language, screen-reader-friendly text version and keyboard-operable controls; W3C’s WCAG 2 guidance is a baseline for accessibility, not a substitute for user testing.[1]

## Case/mechanism: vTaiwan’s online–offline consultation, illustrated by UberX

vTaiwan describes a decentralized consultation process combining online and offline interaction and the use of Pol.is for opinion collection and clustering.[2] An external case study describes an UberX consultation that brought online opinion collection together with a public stakeholder meeting and reports more than 4,500 participants.[3] A study by Moats and Tseng analyzes how platform algorithms and comments shape the apparent public, emphasizing that patterns may contain more nuance than simple pro/anti groupings.[4]

**Interpretation to test:** the design may connect large-scale input to facilitated deliberation and official response. The interface must not imply that algorithmic clusters are public consensus or represent all affected groups, and the Taiwan experience should prompt questions rather than be presented as a portable template.

**Evidence needed / uncertainty:** examine original Uber consultation data, identify who participated and who may have been absent, and trace which recommendations were adopted, altered, or rejected in regulatory records. Reported participation counts and definitions of success vary by source. Sources also differ in perspective: vTaiwan’s self-description, an external case study, and critical scholarship should be shown distinctly.[2][3][4]
