## Website idea: “One decision, one episode”

Build an interactive case page that follows a bounded decision from mandate to public outcome. Visitors scrub through an annotated timeline showing who set the question, what participants heard, how they deliberated, what they recommended, and what elected officials and the public did next. Each stage should link to primary documents and distinguish what participants could decide from what remained with government or voters. End with an evidence-and-limits panel rather than a victory lap. The OECD principles support transparent mandates, recommendations, responses, evaluation, and implementation tracking.[1]

## Case/mechanism: Ireland’s Citizens’ Assembly abortion deliberation (2016–18)

Frame this as the abortion deliberation episode within the Assembly’s five-topic remit, not as a single-issue assembly. The terms of reference specify 99 citizens selected randomly to be broadly representative, the recommendation route through a parliamentary committee and Houses, and a government response to each recommendation.[2] Published scholarship describes five weekends of deliberation on the Eighth Amendment; the Assembly vote and the later referendum were different decisions and ballot questions.[3]

**Evidence to surface:** mandate and response obligations; selection and recruitment information; sessions, expert materials and public submissions; exact Assembly ballot questions/results; committee report and parliamentary response; referendum result; and dated follow-through. Keep recommendation, debate, legislation, referendum, and implementation as distinct steps.

**Uncertainty and counterevidence:** Similar Assembly and referendum results do not establish that the Assembly caused the public vote. Farrell, Suiter, Cunningham, and Harris report that members were substantially in favour of liberalisation at the outset, while some were undecided and the group moved during deliberation.[3] The mandate is evidence of intended design, not a process evaluation; the public case study reports the results but does not demonstrate causality.[2][4] Avoid treating the Irish case as a plug-and-play success model.
